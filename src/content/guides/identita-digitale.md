A **digital identity** is how you actually *use* Italy's public administration online.
Once you have your codice fiscale, residency and health enrolment, almost every
service — INPS, the Agenzia delle Entrate, your **Fascicolo Sanitario Elettronico**
(electronic health record), your comune's online desk, tax payments and public
bonuses — is reached by logging in with one of two national digital identities:
**SPID** or the **CIE**. Both meet the EU **eIDAS** standard.

## Why you need one

Without a digital identity you can do very little online, and end up queueing in
person for things that take two minutes from a phone. With SPID or CIE you can:

- log in to **INPS** (social security) and the **Agenzia delle Entrate** (tax);
- read prescriptions and reports in your **Fascicolo Sanitario Elettronico**, and
  choose or change your *medico di base* online;
- use your **comune's** online services (certificates, appointments, payments);
- claim public benefits and bonuses, and sign documents digitally.

## Option A — SPID (the fast, online route)

**SPID** (*Sistema Pubblico di Identità Digitale*) is a digital identity issued not
by the state directly but by accredited private **Identity Providers** (*gestori*),
overseen by **AgID**. It is the quickest to get and is accepted by the vast majority
of services.

**What you need:** to be 18+, **a valid Italian identity document** — an Italian
*carta d'identità* (including the CIE), an Italian driving licence, or an Italian
passport — plus your **codice fiscale / tessera sanitaria**, an email address, and an
**Italian mobile number**.

> **You don't have to be an Italian citizen** to get SPID — but, per spid.gov.it, the
> identity document must be **Italian-issued**. A home-country passport on its own is
> **not** accepted, and a *permesso di soggiorno* doesn't count as the document. If
> you've just arrived with only a foreign passport, get your **Italian carta d'identità
> (CIE)** at the comune first (Option B): that's the document SPID will accept — and the
> CIE is itself a digital identity you can use directly, so you may not even need SPID.

**How and where:** register online on any Identity Provider's site (the official
list of providers is on spid.gov.it), then verify your identity by one of several
methods — free ones (for example with your CIE/CNS, or a free recorded video
session) or paid ones (a live webcam operator, usually a few euros), or in person at
a partner counter such as a post office.

**Security levels:** Level 1 (password), **Level 2** (password + a one-time code —
what most services ask for), and Level 3 (a physical device). Getting SPID is free;
only some identification methods carry a charge.

## Option B — CIE (your ID card, which is also a digital identity)

The **CIE** (*Carta d'Identità Elettronica*) is the Italian electronic identity card.
It is both your physical ID **and** a built-in digital identity — the
"**Entra con CIE**" login — at the highest assurance level.

**Where and how:** you book at your **comune's Ufficio Anagrafe**, and *how* you book
is set by the comune — its own online form, by phone, or in person. Some comuni use
the Ministry's national *Agenda CIE* portal (`prenotazionicie.interno.gov.it`), but
its citizen area only lets you **log in with a digital identity you already have
(SPID/CIE/CNS)** — there's no plain sign-up, which is a catch-22 for your **first**
Italian ID. So if you don't yet have SPID or a CIE, **book through your comune's own
channel** (check its website, or call/visit the Ufficio Anagrafe). Bring a recent
passport-style photo, your codice fiscale/tessera sanitaria, and your previous ID (for
a renewal).

**Cost and timing:** about **€16.79** plus fixed fees (roughly **€22** in total,
varying slightly by comune). The card is produced by the State Mint and **mailed
within about 6 working days** to the address you choose. Your PIN arrives in two
halves — the first at the counter, the second with the card.

**Using it online:** install the **CieID** app and log in by tapping the card to your
phone (NFC) or through the app, which also offers quicker level-1/2 access. CIE is
recognised across the EU under eIDAS.

## Which one, when, and where

- **Already hold an Italian ID document?** (an Italian ID card, driving licence or
  passport) Then **SPID** is the fastest route: register online, free, no appointment,
  and accepted almost everywhere.
- **Just arrived with only a home-country passport?** SPID won't accept a foreign
  document, so get your **CIE** (Italian ID card) at the comune once you're a resident.
  It is itself a digital identity — so you may not need SPID at all — and if you do want
  SPID later, the CIE unlocks it for free (CIE + PIN).
- **Sensible order for most newcomers:** residency → **CIE** → (optionally) SPID. For
  anyone arriving without an Italian document, the CIE is the foundational digital
  identity; SPID is the quick add-on once you hold one.
- **Looking ahead:** the government has announced a gradual move toward **CIE as the
  single national digital identity** over the coming years, with SPID wound down — so
  CIE is the longer-term choice anyway.

## Common pitfalls

- **No Italian mobile number.** SPID registration and its one-time codes need one —
  arrange a SIM early.
- **Mismatched details.** Your name and codice fiscale must match across your
  documents, or identity verification fails.
- **The CIE PIN halves.** Keep both the slip from the counter and the part that
  arrives with the card; you need both to set the PIN.
- **Settling for Level 1.** Most services require **Level 2**, so set that up rather
  than password-only access.

## You're set up

With a digital identity in hand you can run the rest from a phone: book anagrafe and
ASL appointments, read your health record, deal with INPS and the tax office, and
claim what you are entitled to — the practical pay-off of the first three steps.
