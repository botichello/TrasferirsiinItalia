EU/EEA driving licences are legally **equivalent to Italian ones** (art.
136-bis of the Codice della Strada): taking residency in Italy does not, by
itself, oblige you to do anything about your licence. But once you are
resident, Italian rules on validity and renewal apply — and that is where
newcomers get caught out.

## While your licence is valid: nothing to do

A licence with a standard EU expiry (10–15 years for categories AM/A/B, 5 for
C/D, per Directive 2006/126/EC) stays valid to drive in Italy **until the
expiry date printed on it** (field 4b). You may convert it earlier if you
want — it is optional.

The one exception is the **2-year rule**: a licence with **no expiry date**,
or with a validity longer than EU norms allow, must be converted within **2
years of taking residence** in Italy.

## When it expires: conversion, not renewal

Once you are an Italian resident, your home country can no longer renew your
licence — renewal belongs to your country of *normal residence* (where you
live at least 185 days a year). Instead, you **convert** (*conversione*) the
licence into an Italian one at any **Ufficio della Motorizzazione Civile**,
with no exam:

1. If the licence is expiring or expired, get a **medical fitness check**
   from an authorised doctor (*medico abilitato*) — the receipt must be no
   more than 3 months old when you file.
2. Pay via **PagoPA**, tariff N003: €10.20 + €32.00 (different tariffs apply
   in Sicily, Valle d'Aosta, Trentino-Alto Adige and Friuli-Venezia Giulia).
3. Fill in form **TT2112** and submit it with the documents below at the
   Motorizzazione. Your foreign licence is withdrawn and returned to the
   issuing state; you receive an Italian licence.

> **Conversion is irreversible** — once the Italian licence is issued, the
> foreign one has been sent back and you cannot opt out again.

After conversion you follow the Italian validity clock (10 years for a B
licence, shorter over age 50/70/80) and the Italian renewal ritual: a medical
check, €26.20 in fees, and a new card by insured mail.

## If you let it lapse

An expired EU/EEA licence can **always** be converted, but the longer you
wait the harder it gets:

- expired **less than 5 years**: medical certificate plus checks that it was
  not suspended or revoked;
- expired **more than 5 years**: you must also pass a practical driving check
  (*esperimento di guida*) before the Italian licence is handed over.

Driving on an expired licence while resident is sanctioned like driving on an
expired Italian one (art. 126 CdS).

## Common pitfalls

- **Trying to renew with your home authority** after moving — renewal must
  happen in your country of normal residence, i.e. Italy.
- **Ignoring the 2-year deadline** if your licence has no expiry date.
- **Letting the licence lapse past 5 years** — that adds a driving check to
  the process.
- **Medical certificate timing** — older than 3 months at filing and it is
  refused.
