> Verified against EU free-movement rules and official guidance (Ministero
> dell'Interno / Anagrafe Nazionale, Your Europe). The **local** procedure —
> office, booking system and forms — varies by comune, so check your city's page
> or your Comune's website for the specifics.

For stays longer than **three months**, EU/EEA citizens are required to register
their residency at the **anagrafe** (registry office) of the comune where they
live. This is the EU equivalent of "getting your papers in order" — and, unlike
non-EU citizens, you do **not** need a residence permit to do it.

## What you must demonstrate

EU free-movement rules let you register if you fall into one of these categories:

- you are **employed or self-employed** in Italy; or
- you have **sufficient economic resources** for yourself (and family) **plus
  health insurance cover**; or
- you are **enrolled in study** and have health cover and resources.

You prove the relevant condition with supporting documents (a work contract, proof
of income, enrolment, insurance, etc.).

## The process

1. Apply at your comune's anagrafe with your ID, codice fiscale, proof of your
   category, and proof of address.
2. The comune processes the request and arranges a **residency-address check** —
   typically a visit by the local police (*vigili*) to confirm you live at the
   stated address.
3. Once confirmed, you are entered in the population register and can obtain an
   **attestazione di iscrizione anagrafica**.

## Why it matters

Registered residency is what unlocks enrolment in the national health service
(Step 3), and is the reference point for tax residency, your *carta d'identità*,
and many local services.

## Next step

Once you are a registered resident, you can enrol with the regional health
service and choose a *medico di base* (family doctor).
