With your **codice fiscale** in hand (and ideally your residency registered), an
Italian bank account is what makes the rest of daily life work: salary, rent,
utilities, and most subscriptions in Italy expect an Italian **IBAN** with
direct debits.

## Your EU right to an account

Under EU payment-account rules (Directive 2014/92/EU, implemented in Italy by
d.lgs. 37/2017 in the Testo Unico Bancario), **every consumer legally residing
in the EU has the right to a basic payment account** — in Italian, the **conto
di base** — regardless of nationality or where in the EU they live. Banks and
Poste Italiane are obliged to offer it, and a bank cannot refuse you just
because you are not (yet) an Italian resident.

A refusal is lawful only on anti-money-laundering grounds or because you
already hold an equivalent payment account in Italy — and it must be given to
you **in writing, free of charge, within 10 working days**.

## Conto di base vs. an ordinary account

The **conto di base** bundles a preset package of operations (incoming and
outgoing transfers, salary or pension crediting, a debit card for payments and
ATM withdrawals) into one capped annual fee. By design it cannot include an
overdraft, a chequebook, or a linked securities account.

It is **free of charge** if you hold a valid **ISEE** (the household
means-test indicator) **not above €11,600** — in that case it is also exempt
from stamp duty — and free (stamp duty still due) for pensioners with gross
pensions up to €18,000 a year.

An **ordinary conto corrente** is at the bank's discretion and is what most
working residents end up with: richer features, app-based banking, and fees
that vary widely — compare before choosing.

## How to open one

1. **Get your codice fiscale first** (Step 1). Banks are legally required to
   record it for every customer relationship (DPR 605/1973, art. 7), so
   without it you will be turned away.
2. **Compare accounts.** Every bank must hand you the standardised EU fee
   document (*Documento informativo sulle spese*) before you sign — ask for
   it, it is free and carries no commitment.
3. **Apply at a branch or online** with your ID and codice fiscale. The bank
   runs its identification checks and takes a specimen signature.
4. **Sign and keep the contract** together with the *Documento di Sintesi*
   (the terms summary).
5. You receive your **IBAN** and debit card — set up salary crediting and
   utility direct debits from there.

## The stamp duty (imposta di bollo)

Italy levies a government stamp duty of **€34.20 per year** on individuals'
current accounts — but **only when the average balance** (*giacenza media*)
of your accounts at that bank **exceeds €5,000**. Keep less than that on
account and no bollo is due.

## Common pitfalls

- **Asking for "an account" when you qualify for the conto di base.** Branch
  staff rarely volunteer it — request it by name.
- **Opening a conto di base while already holding another Italian payment
  account.** That is a lawful ground for refusal (unless you are switching).
- **Forgetting the annual ISEE renewal** for the free version — miss it and
  fees and stamp duty resume.
- **Expecting an overdraft or chequebook on a conto di base.** Both are
  excluded by design; you need an ordinary account for those.
